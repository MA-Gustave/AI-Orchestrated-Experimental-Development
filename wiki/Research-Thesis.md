# Research thesis

## Central proposition

When a technical layer becomes reliable enough to be operated through a stable interface, human work **may shift** toward specifying objectives, composing subsystems, supervising execution, and evaluating outcomes. Automation does not destroy complexity. It redistributes it among software, tools, interfaces, infrastructure, and human responsibility.

Generative AI may extend this shift from code translation toward the production and evaluation of candidate implementations. The case study is an experimental RISC-V Vector JSON parser research lab directed through a human-defined research process.

## Three contributions, kept distinct

1. **Conceptual argument:** automation can change which level of a technical system a human can productively operate.
2. **Public methodological account:** a pattern library, explicit candidate configurations, native validation, exploratory measurements, reports, and revisions form a reusable experimental workflow.
3. **Future architectural hypothesis:** optional workload descriptors may help choose specialized JSON parsing strategies, subject to correctness and end-to-end cost constraints.

## What the project does not claim

- It does not establish that programming expertise is obsolete or that AI writes superior code in all settings.
- It does not claim the invention of abstraction, algorithm selection, automated program synthesis, or autotuning.
- It does not prove that AI orchestration caused the measured performance improvements; no controlled human-vs-AI study was conducted.
- It does not claim a production-ready runtime selector across all candidate binaries or a standardized JSON profiling format.

**Research question:** Can AI-assisted experimental orchestration expand the technical search capacity of an operator while maintaining transparent validity, attribution, and reproducible evidence?
